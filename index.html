01

What this actually is

Before a company lists, everyone involved — the promoters, the auditor, the merchant banker — runs their own version of the same review: are the numbers healthy, is the paperwork complete, are there legal skeletons, does governance hold up, and roughly what should this be worth. IPOReady AI puts that review in one place and scores it consistently instead of leaving it as a set of separate spreadsheets and gut calls.

It works from whatever data you give it — a filled-in workbook or numbers typed in by hand — and produces a live dashboard, a gap list, a what-if simulator for testing how a decision would move the numbers, and a preliminary draft document you can hand to your actual advisors.

None of it is a substitute for that advisor. It's a rehearsal, not the filing.

How it works

Four stages, from raw numbers to a document your team can actually review.

01

Bring your numbers

Upload a workbook or type in three years of financials, company and IPO details, and self-reported legal and governance answers.

02

The engine reads it

Ratios are computed year over year — margins, leverage, interest coverage, working-capital days — and checked against a 10-item disclosure checklist.

03

Get a readiness score

Eight categories, each weighted by how much it actually matters for listing, sum to one overall number out of 95 or 100.

04

Generate the draft

A preliminary IPO information document is assembled from your data — company overview through risk factors — downloadable as Word.

03

Two ways in

The app behaves differently depending on which upload you use — not just in what it accepts, but in how the score itself is built.

SCORED OUT OF 95

Listed companies, several at once

  • Who it's for — merchant bankers, auditors, or due-diligence teams screening a handful of already-listed companies side by side.
  • The file — one Excel workbook, one sheet per company, tabs named "Company 1", "Company 2" and so on. Figures go in as plain rupees and convert to ₹ crore automatically.
  • Why it drops a category — a company that's already listed has nothing left to "prepare" for its own IPO, so IPO Preparation is removed entirely rather than scored as zero. The total becomes 95, not 100.
  • Loading it in — upload the workbook, pick a company from the dropdown, then load it. The score only updates once you do.
spacer

What still travels with each sheet

  • Company info, the disclosure checklist, legal and governance answers, industry risk, and a peer valuation multiple can all sit on the same sheet as the financials, and load in alongside them.
  • An IPO Preparation row is allowed in the sheet for reference but is always ignored here — whether the category counts is decided by which upload route you used, not by anything written on the sheet itself.
  • Two tabs sharing the same company name simply overwrite each other, and any tab that isn't named "Company…" or has no valid financial table is skipped without an error.
SCORED OUT OF 100

A single company preparing to list

  • Who it's for — an unlisted, IPO-bound company checking its own readiness before approaching a banker.
  • The file — either one Excel sheet with everything (company info, financials, disclosures, legal, governance, valuation) or a plain CSV holding just the 12-column financial table.
  • Why nothing drops — preparation genuinely matters here, so IPO Preparation stays in and is worth 5 points, scored by how many of four fields — Issue Size, Fresh Issue, Offer for Sale, Use of Funds — are actually filled in.
  • Loading it in — it loads the moment you upload it; there's no company-selection step, since it's always exactly one company.
spacer

One thing worth knowing about CSV

  • A CSV upload is financials only — no company info, disclosures, or IPO details ride along with it, so those still need to be filled in by hand afterward.
  • CSV values are also not auto-converted the way Excel figures are — enter them already in ₹ crore, not plain rupees.
  • Everything else about the scoring — the 100-point total, the four-field IPO Preparation check — behaves identically whether the data arrived as Excel or CSV.

For a company loaded through the Banker / Auditor route, IPO Preparation is removed from this table entirely — not scored as zero, removed — so the total that's actually being summed becomes 95. Every other weighting stays exactly as shown.

Financial Health

25 pts

Starts at full marks for the latest year and loses points for high leverage, thin net margins, weak or negative revenue growth, and weak interest coverage.

Debt & Risk

20 pts

Weighted toward the debt-to-equity ratio, with a further deduction if receivables are taking longer than 90 days to collect.

Disclosure Completeness

15 pts

The share of the 10-item checklist marked Complete, with half-credit given to anything still marked Needs Review.

Legal & Compliance

15 pts

Four self-reported flags: pending litigation, unresolved tax disputes, past regulatory action, and whether related-party transactions are properly disclosed. Answers left as "Not disclosed" score as unresolved.

Governance & Auditor Quality

10 pts

Whether the auditor is reputable, an audit committee exists, at least a third of directors are independent, and there's been no qualified audit opinion in the last three years.

Financial Consistency

5 pts

How many of the figures entered by hand match the same figures pulled from source documents, via the inconsistency detector.

Corporate Information

5 pts

Whether the basic company profile — name, industry, promoter names — has actually been filled in at all.

IPO Preparation

5 pts

Unlisted companies only. Counts how many of four fields — Issue Size, Fresh Issue, Offer for Sale, Use of Funds — are filled in: all four earns full marks, three earns 4/5, two earns 2/5, one earns 1/5.

06

What's inside

Six working pages behind the dashboard, each answering a different question.

Financial Health

Year-over-year revenue, EBITDA and PAT trends, margin and leverage charts, working-capital days, and a plain-English read of each ratio in the latest year — not just the number, but what it usually means.

Gap & Risk Detector

The 10-item disclosure checklist, editable inline, plus an inconsistency detector: enter the same figure as it appears in two places — what you typed in versus what a source document shows — and it flags anything that doesn't match by more than ₹0.01 crore.

Legal, Governance & Valuation

Self-reported legal flags, auditor and board-governance quality, customer-concentration risk, an illustrative pass/fail check against a handful of commonly cited SEBI eligibility criteria, and a rough pre-IPO valuation range from PAT and a peer P/E multiple.

What-If Simulator

Move sliders for a change in debt, an assumed interest rate, and a change in revenue, and watch debt-to-equity, interest expense, PAT and the overall readiness score move with it — before anything actually changes.

Draft Generator

Assembles an eight-section preliminary IPO information document — overview, business model, promoters, financial summary, ratios, disclosure status, risk factors, use of proceeds — straight from whatever data is currently loaded, downloadable as a Word file.

Data Input

Where both routes live: the multi-company Banker/Auditor upload, the single-company upload, downloadable templates for each, and a manual-entry panel for anything a file doesn't cover.

What this isn't

IPOReady AI is a simulation and prototyping tool — a rehearsal for the review a merchant banker, auditor, or legal counsel will actually run, not a replacement for any of them.

The SEBI eligibility check covers a handful of commonly cited criteria that can be read straight from a set of financial statements — it is not the complete rulebook, and real eligibility should be verified with a merchant banker. The valuation range is a starting point for a conversation, not a valuation opinion. Every score and every draft this produces is preliminary, and should be reviewed by the people who will actually be accountable for the filing before it goes anywhere near one.
